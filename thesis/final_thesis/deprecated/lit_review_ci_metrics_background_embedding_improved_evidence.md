| RAG retrieves documents and conditions generation on them (canonical RAG definition). | “We endow pre-trained, parametric-memory generation models with a non-parametric memory through a general-purpose fine-tuning approach which we refer to as retrieval-augmented generation (RAG). … The retriever (Dense Passage Retriever [26], henceforth DPR) provides latent documents conditioned on the input, and the seq2seq model (BART [32]) then conditions on these latent documents together with the input to generate the output.” | Lewis et al. | Commercial/web LLM interfaces use search/RAG-style retrieval in their web products. | “Most commercial LLMs, like ChatGPT [OpenAI, 2023], Gemini [Gemini Team, 2023], and Perplex-ity.ai, already employ RAG in their Web interfaces. For example, ChatGPT employs a Bing search, whereas Gemini accesses Google Search results.” | Wu et al. (2024/2025 v3), *ClashEval* (`https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.10198`) | Introduction |
| Retrieved context can *overconstrain/mislead* generation: models may adopt incorrect retrieved content even when their prior knowledge is correct. | “we find that LLMs are susceptible to adopting incorrect re-trieved content, overriding their own correct prior knowledge over 60% of the time.” | Wu et al. (2024/2025 v3), *ClashEval* (`https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.10198`) | Abstract |
| More retrieved context can be a trade-off; models don’t robustly use long contexts and can struggle when relevant evidence is in the middle; extra retrieved docs can have diminishing returns. | “We find that changing the position of relevant information in the input context can substantially affect model performance, indicating that current language models do not robustly access and use information in long input contexts.” … “Our results indicate that prompting language models with longer input contexts is a trade-off—providing the language model with more information may help it perform the downstream task, but it also increases the amount of content that the model must reason over, potentially decreasing accuracy.” … “current models fail to effectively use additional retrieved documents—using 50 documents instead of 20 retrieved documents only marginally improves performance (∼1.5% for GPT-3.5-Turbo and ∼1% for claude-1.3).” | Liu et al. (2023), *Lost in the Middle: How Language Models Use Long Contexts* (`https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.03172`) | Early paper results section / summary of findings |
| Embedding similarity can miss nuanced semantic relationships (e.g., negation/critical differences) and overestimate similarity for textually-similar but semantically-different pairs. | “The models fail to distinguish and decouple textual similarity and semantic similarity. And they may overestimate the semantic similarity of any pairs with similar textual regardless of the actual semantic difference between them.” … “Soft negative samples share highly similar textual but have surely and apparently different semantic with the original samples. Specifically, we take the negation of original sentences as soft negative samples …” | Wang et al. (2022), *SNCSE: Contrastive Learning for Unsupervised Sentence Embedding with Soft Negative Samples* (`https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.05979`) | Introduction |
| Direct source attribution is hard because LLM generation can mix content from multiple sources; even one word may originate from multiple documents. | “the generated content mainly consists of multiple words from different sources; even a single word could originate from multiple documents.” … “For example, the source of the sentence ”apple is delicious.” would be multiple documents.” … “For example, ”Apple is a company and is delicious.” is a mixture of contents in two documents.” | Park & Choi (2024), *Identifying the Source of Generation for Large Language Models* (`https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.12846`) | Introduction |
| Also, standard pretraining doesn’t preserve source metadata (so models can’t directly provide “where this came from” without extra machinery). | “In pretraining, LLM trains to maximize the likeli-hood of text but neither receives the source of the text nor memorizes the source. Accordingly, LLM can not provide document information on the generated content …” | Park & Choi (2024), *Identifying the Source of Generation for Large Language Models* (`https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.12846`) | Abstract |
| In RAG, internal model knowledge can conflict with retrieved external knowledge; this can yield hallucinated answers that don’t match cited/referenced documents. | “the internal knowledge of the LLM may conflict with the external knowledge, resulting in a hallucinated answer (Xie et al., 2024). As a consequence, the answer does not match the referenced documents;” | Batista et al. (2025), *SAFE / Think Before You Attribute…* (`https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.12621`) | Background section (RAG vs attribution categories) |
| “Confirmation bias” (relevant to only searching for supporting evidence) can be described as selectively seeking supportive evidence and neglecting disconfirming evidence. | “one selectively looks for data that are supportive of the hypothesis and neglects to look for disconfirmatory data …” | Nickerson (1998), *Confirmation Bias: A Ubiquitous Phenomenon in Many Guises* (`http://psy.ucsd.edu/~mckenzie/nickersonConfirmationBias.pdf`) | Main text (medicine/diagnosis discussion) |
